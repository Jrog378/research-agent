# Candidate research opportunities

Run: 2026-10-05-literature-01. These are study proposals, not verified novel contributions.

## A — Evaluate a reproducible disruption indicator (recommended pilot)

Question: Can a simple seasonally adjusted weekly grain-movement indicator identify independently documented disruption periods without excessive false alerts?

Start with one Locks 27 movement series (D03), verify measurement units, and reserve later periods for evaluation. Compare same-season historical and rolling baselines using only earlier observations. Examine low and high water separately; do not assume every decline is drought.

Prior work: P05 already analyzes disruption consequences; D01/D02 already monitor activity. Potential contribution to investigate: validated alert performance, reporting delays and revisions, and transfer across periods. Search novelty remains unresolved.

Validation: independently sourced dated navigation restrictions/closures where available, with an explicit event-label rubric. USACE-derived USDA flows are not independent validation of USACE tonnage. Water levels provide environmental context, not proof of disruption. Report event precision/recall, false alerts, detection timing, missingness and threshold sensitivity. If records contain only final revised data, call this retrospective detection, not real-time performance.

What could weaken it: alerts simply reflect harvest/demand; event labels are incomplete; missing reports create anomalies; baseline wins no advantage; restricted sites do not correspond to the measured corridor. Feasibility unresolved until workbook and event history are examined.

## B — Freight-proxy validity

Question: How stable is the relationship between vessel/barge activity and reported tonnage across locations and environmental regimes?

P01–P04 establish related measurement approaches; D02 already estimates tonnage. Candidate extension: geographic/temporal transfer and uncertainty calibration. Requires matched observations, loading information and an independent reference. AIS access and labelled data are not verified. More demanding than A; reject if reference coverage is inadequate or prior work already provides the proposed evaluation.

## C — Agent auditability

Question: Can a bounded literature agent reliably distinguish established capabilities from candidate gaps and unsupported novelty claims?

The present repository supplies a workflow, not experimental evidence. A study would require an expert-labelled paper set, prespecified scoring, repeated runs, a comparable human-directed baseline and consistent search budgets. Could fit the course's agent emphasis but agent-evaluation literature must be searched separately before claiming contribution.
